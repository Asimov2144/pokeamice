  translation: 第1章：创作者的音乐原点——从武满彻、古典管弦到摇滚与合成器
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: First, may I inquire about your musical roots, Shinji Miyazaki-san?
  translation: 首先，能否请宫崎慎二先生谈谈您的音乐根源？
- speaker: 宫崎慎二
  original: Thinking back, I’ve trivially listened to music since I was small. There
    was a slightly rich boy in our neighborhood (laughs) and he had a shortwave radio
    at home. And whenever I visited the boy at home, we would listen to the Billboard
    music of that time. I remember listening to The Spotnicks and many other things.
  translation: 回想起来，我从小便不经意地听着音乐。我们街区有个家境稍显富裕的男孩（笑），他家有一台短波收音机。每次去他家玩，我们就会一起听当时的公告牌音乐。我记得听过The
    Spotnicks乐队的曲子，还有许多其他作品。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: So you mostly listened to Western music?
  translation: 所以您主要听的是西洋音乐吗？
- speaker: 宫崎慎二
  original: No, I also listened to Kazuo Funaki-san and Sylvie Vartan’s “Search for
    the Idol” (Cherchez l’idole) and other things.
  translation: 不，我也听了船木一夫先生和西尔维·瓦尔坦的《寻找偶像》（Cherchez l'idole）等作品。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: And what made you want to become a professional?
  translation: 那么，是什么促使您立志成为职业作曲家的呢？
- speaker: 宫崎慎二
  original: I liked music, but I hadn’t received any specialized education on it,
    so I tried to attend a normal university. However, I couldn’t give up on music,
    so I worried myself, wondering if there were any connections. Shortly before that,
    I just happened to listen to Toru Takemitsu-san’s music and was incredibly moved.
    I also read Takemitsu-san’s literary work and thought, “This person is amazing.”
    That’s when I started brooding and went all the way to Tokyo to meet him.
  translation: 我虽然喜爱音乐，却未曾接受过任何专业教育，于是便打算报考普通大学。然而，终究无法割舍对音乐的执念，我为此苦恼不已，思忖着是否有什么门路可寻。恰在彼时，我偶然聆听了武满彻先生的音乐，深受震撼。又读了武满先生的著作，心想“此人真是了不起”。于是我开始郁郁思索，一路奔赴东京去见他。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: What, all of a sudden (laughs)?
  translation: 怎么突然这么问？（笑）
- speaker: 宫崎慎二
  original: Yes (laughs). I visited a reporter who had written an article on Takemitsu-san
    in the newspaper and told him that I’d like to meet him. But I was told that he
    was not in Japan but in Paris at the moment, attending a music festival or something.
    There was nothing to be done about it, so I thought, “What now? Should I go home?”
    and was just lazing around at Yotsuya station, when this poster from a music academy
    caught my eye. Not to mention, it said, “basic course”. I wondered, “What do you
    do in a basic course? Could it be something I could do as well?” And so I ordered
    an application form and… All in all, I listened to different kinds of music and
    just dimly thought, “It would be nice to enter a world like this”, but it wasn’t
    until I was twenty that I approached it concretely.
  translation: 是的（笑）。我曾拜访过一位在报纸上撰写武满彻先生相关文章的记者，告诉他我想与武满先生见一面。却被告知他此刻不在日本，而是在巴黎参加某个音乐节之类的活动。事已至此，我心想“这下该怎么办？要打道回府吗？”，正百无聊赖地待在四谷站时，一张音乐学院的海报映入了我的眼帘。而且上面还写着“基础课程”。我寻思着“基础课程到底是做什么的？莫非我也能学得来？”于是便索要了报名资料……总而言之，我听了各种各样的音乐，只是朦胧地想着“若能踏入这样的世界该多好”，但真正具体地付诸行动，已经是二十岁时候的事了。
- type: heading
  level: 2
  original: 'Chapter 2: Game Freak''s Origins — Soundtracks, Brass Bands, and Chiptunes'
  translation: 第2章：GAME FREAK 的根基——电影原声、吹奏乐部与8位元点阵之魂
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Where do the musical roots of the Pokémon sound team lie?
  translation: 宝可梦声音团队的音乐根基源自何处？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: I was in third or fourth grade in elementary school. We had the soundtracks
    of “The Bridge on the River Kwai” and “Ben Hur” at home and I remember listening
    to them and trying to emulate them with my recorder. I feel like that was the
    first moment in which I thought, “Music is interesting!” Other than that, there
    was the YMO (Yellow Magic Orchestra) I listened to when I was in fifth or sixth
    grade. From then on, I became more of a techno type. Tangerine Dream, Ultravox,
    Kraftwerk and such. My parents bought me a radio-cassette player, but I only listened
    to rock and Western music. That’s why there was almost no one in school I could
    relate to (laughs). Not to mention, records were expensive so you couldn’t buy
    them so easily.
  translation: 我那时正读小学三、四年级。家里有《桂河大桥》和《宾虚》的原声唱片，我记得自己听着它们，试着用竖笛去模仿。我觉得那是我第一次感到“音乐真有趣！”除此之外，还有五、六年级时听的YMO（Yellow
    Magic Orchestra）。从那以后，我变得更偏向电子乐一派。Tangerine Dream、Ultravox、Kraftwerk之类的。父母给我买了收录机，但我只听摇滚和西方音乐。所以学校里几乎没人能和我聊到一块去（笑）。更何况唱片很贵，也不是那么容易就能买得起的。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: So that’s when the radio airchecks came in, right (laughs)?
  translation: 于是电台播出的录音就派上用场了，对吧（笑）？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: Yes. But even when a song was played, I didn’t know who sang it and what
    is was called. That’s why I bought magazines and read them to get information.
    That’s how it was back then.
  translation: 是的。但即便有歌曲播放，我也既不知道演唱者是谁，也不知道曲名为何。正因如此，我才去买杂志翻阅，从中获取信息。当时就是这样的时代。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: For you, Masuda-san, what era constitutes the base of your music works
    today?
  translation: 对增田先生而言，您如今音乐创作的根基，主要建立在哪个时代的作品之上呢？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: It was when I entered junior high school, I think. I had pestered my parents
    into buying a synthesizer called MS-10 for me. And since there was someone at
    school who liked those things as well, we used our two cassette decks to do multiple
    recordings together (laughs). So I never actually studied music as my specialty.
  translation: 我想那应该是我升上初中的时候。我缠着父母给我买了一台叫MS-10的合成器。学校里正好也有喜欢这些东西的人，我们就用两台卡带录音机一起做多重录音（笑）。所以我其实并没有把音乐当作专业来正经学过。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Is that so?
  translation: 是这样吗？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: In high school, I was part of a brass band and played the trombone, but
    I never studied it (laughs). Basically, I like techno and rock. Those are far
    away from the Pokémon world. That one goes more in the direction of Marilyn Manson
    and Slipknot.
  translation: 高中时我曾加入铜管乐队，吹奏长号，但从没正经学过（笑）。基本上，我偏爱电子乐与摇滚。那些跟宝可梦的世界相距甚远。宝可梦的世界更偏向玛丽莲·曼森和活结乐队那个方向。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: Masuda-san’s taste in music from back then is reflected in the battle
    tracks (laughs).
  translation: 增田先生当时的音乐品味，在战斗曲目中体现得淋漓尽致（笑）。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Where do your musical roots lie then, Ichinose-san?
  translation: 那么，一之濑先生，你的音乐根基在哪里呢？
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: When I was in kindergarten, I was forced to attend a swimming school and
    a music class. As for the swimming school, I kept crying until I didn’t have to
    attend any more, but I remained in the music class the whole time. It’s just that
    I never did my homework, so my music teacher told me, “I won’t let you advance
    to higher classes.” That’s when I switched to the piano. But even then, I didn’t
    practice unless it was a piece I liked. That went on until junior high school,
    I think. In terms of piano [music], I liked Chopin the most. I often thought,
    “I want to play this piece!” and put a lot of effort into practicing a difficult
    piece. It was also during junior high school that I got into game music. Famicom
    [Family Computer] music, Game Center music and things like that. I also took recording
    devices to the Game Center and recorded the sounds, then played them on the piano.
  translation: 上幼儿园时，我曾被强制送去游泳学校和音乐教室。游泳学校那边，我一直哭闹，直到终于不用再去为止；而音乐教室则一直坚持了下来。只是我从来不做作业，音乐老师便对我说：“我不会让你升入更高年级。”于是我便转而改学钢琴。可即便如此，若不是自己喜欢的曲子，我也还是不练。这种情况大概一直持续到初中吧。就钢琴[曲目]而言，我最喜欢肖邦。我常常想着“我想弹这首曲子！”，然后为练习一首高难度的作品付出很多努力。也是在上初中期间，我开始迷上了游戏音乐。红白机[Family
    Computer]的音乐、游戏厅的音乐之类的。我还会带着录音设备去游戏厅把声音录下来，然后在钢琴上弹奏。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Hearing your story, it sounds like you became part of Game Freak because
    you were destined to do so.
  translation: 听您这么一说，倒像是您命中注定要加入GAME FREAK似的。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: 'But the thing is, at that time, I wasn’t even thinking about making music.
    I only entered Game Freak because I wanted to acquire some qualifications. I just
    happened to find they were recruiting people for part-time jobs and said, “Excuse
    me, please let me study here.” So I studied programming, but I couldn’t seem to
    make progress and before I knew it, I was making music (laughs). Also, I’ve just
    remembered something else that lead to my starting with music: Once while we were
    moving, I discovered a record for prenatal care in our house. Apparently, it was
    music I used to hear when I was still an unborn child; when I checked the playlist,
    I realized it was full of pieces that I had selected to play on the piano.'
  translation: 不过，当时我压根没想过要去做音乐。我进GAME FREAK，纯粹是想考个资格证。碰巧看到他们在招兼职，就厚着脸皮说“不好意思，请让我在这里学习”。于是我学了编程，但怎么都学不出名堂，不知不觉间就开始做音乐了（笑）。还有一件事，也是我走上音乐之路的契机：有一次搬家，我在家里翻出一张胎教唱片。那似乎是我还在娘胎里时听的音乐；我一看曲目，发现全是我自己选来用钢琴弹的曲子。
- speaker: 佐藤仁美
  original: Does that mean you had listened to Chopin?
  translation: 这是否意味着您曾聆听过肖邦的作品？
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: Yeah. Chopin and Liszt and such. That’s why I think prenatal care does
    have an effect (laughs).
  translation: 是的。肖邦、李斯特那些。所以我觉得胎教确实是有影响的（笑）。
- speaker: 佐藤仁美
  original: I had a similar experience. Apparently, when I was still 0, my father
    constantly let me listen to a record that contained yodeling. He told me that
    one morning, before I had learned to talk, I was suddenly standing on my futon
    and yodeling (laughs). That’s when my parents began to hope that “this kid may
    have musical talent” (laughs). I began playing the piano at age 3 and continued
    all the way until second grade in high school. Also, since I always went to Christian-oriented
    schools, I was very familiar with hymns. That’s why I listened to little else
    but hymns and piano music until I was 17 or 18 years old (laughs).
  translation: 我也有过类似的经历。据说我还在0岁的时候，父亲就不断地让我听一张含有约德尔调的唱片。他告诉我，某天早晨，在我还没学会说话之前，我突然站在被褥上约德尔了起来（笑）。从那时起，父母便开始期望“这孩子或许有音乐天赋”（笑）。我从3岁开始学钢琴，一直学到高中二年级。此外，由于我一直就读于基督教方向的学校，对赞美诗非常熟悉。所以直到17、18岁为止，我几乎只听过赞美诗和钢琴曲（笑）。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: How did you end up working for Game Freak?
  translation: 您是如何进入GAME FREAK工作的呢？
- speaker: 佐藤仁美
  original: There was a job advertisement stating that Game Freak was looking for
    planners. I worked as a planner for about 7 or 8 years, but then I happened to
    hear that someone from the sound staff had quit. I had told them once that I could
    play the piano and when I was asked if I wanted to give it a try, I said yes.
  translation: 当时有招聘启事说GAME FREAK在招策划。我做了大约七八年策划，后来碰巧听说声音团队有人辞职了。我以前跟他们提过我会弹钢琴，当他们问我愿不愿意试一试时，我就答应了。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: Well, Sato had amazing hearing, you see.
  translation: 这个嘛，佐藤的听力可是相当了得。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Regarding the fact that neither Ichinose-san nor Sato-san were originally
    part of the sound team; does Game Freak often make personnel changes among members?
  translation: 关于一之濑先生与佐藤先生原本并非声音团队成员一事，GAME FREAK 是否经常在成员之间进行人事调整？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: No, it’s not personnel changes; it’s more like we approach people who
    are familiar with music and ask them, “Hey, do you want to try and make a music
    piece like this?” When we find someone talented, we slowly tell them to “come
    over here” (laughs). So it’s not like they change sections and become part of
    the sound team.
  translation: 不，并非人事变动，更像是我们去接触那些熟悉音乐的人，问他们：“嘿，要不要试着创作一首这样的曲子？”当我们发现有才华的人，就会慢慢地对他说“到这边来吧”（笑）。所以并不是他们换了部门、成为声音团队的一员。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: We say, “We’re a bit busy, wanna help us out?” That’s what it feels like.
  translation: 我们常说：“我们有点忙不过来，要不要来帮把手？”感觉就是这样。
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: After we let them produce something, we tell them that they’re talented.
  translation: 在我们让他们创作出一些东西之后，我们会告诉他们，他们很有才华。

- type: image
  image: /assets/img/interviews/2010-08-04-interview-cd-anime-ost-miyazaki-soundteam/cd_cover.jpg

- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: On this CD, all music pieces from the games that were arranged by an orchestra
    and used for the TV anime are recorded, though it seems like some pieces are used
    differently in the anime than in the games.
  translation: 在这张CD中，收录了所有由管弦乐编曲并用于电视动画的游戏乐曲，不过其中似乎也有一些曲目在动画中的使用方式与游戏里有所不同。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: You’re right. For example, Disc 2, Track 9 “Crossing the Ocean”. It’s
    a track from “Ruby & Sapphire” and originally plays while you move from a certain
    place to a town with a boat. It’s a lively piece that feels speedy because of
    the boat motif, but during the game, it’s only played for a very short time. In
    the anime, that piece was turned into a magnificent and moving tune. I thought,
    “This is great! How interesting!”
  translation: 说得没错。比如Disc 2第9首《穿越海洋》。那是《红宝石·蓝宝石》中的曲子，原本是在乘船从某处前往城镇时播放的。因为带有船的意象，是一首轻快而有速度感的乐曲，但在游戏中只播放了很短一段时间。到了动画里，这首曲子被改编成了宏大而动人的旋律。我当时心想：“太棒了！真有意思！”
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: In the case of the anime, a menu (list of the tracks needed) is written
    beforehand to fit certain scenes and characters. How are they [music pieces] produced
    in the case of the games?
  translation: 就动画而言，会事先根据特定场景与角色写好菜单（所需曲目清单）。那么在游戏这边，乐曲又是如何制作出来的呢？
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: First we bring the necessary scenes to light and fortify what kind of
    image we want to give off in them, but as the production of the game continues,
    instances where we say, “I want a tune like this” occur as well, as it was the
    case with the aforementioned boat scene. So instead of determining how we imagine
    everything beforehand, we often let the people in charge of the sound actually
    play the game and then have them produce the tunes.
  translation: 首先，我们会把必要的场景梳理出来，并强化我们希望在这些场景中呈现的意象；但随着游戏制作推进，也会出现像刚才提到的乘船场景那样，直接提出“我想要这样的曲子”的情况。因此，并不是一开始就把一切都设想好，而往往是先让负责声音的人实际游玩游戏，再请他们创作乐曲。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: Being able to produce while looking at the screen would be the most ideal
    way, but since scenes are still being produced during the development phase, we
    work with just our imaginations sometimes.
  translation: 能够一边看着画面一边进行制作是最理想的，但在开发阶段画面本身仍在制作之中，所以有时只能凭借想象来创作。
- speaker: 增田顺一

  translation: 对我来说，是Disc 2的第34首《白岱森林》。原曲是以“森林”的意象创作的，但当我听到编曲版本时，它变成了一首如湖泊般的乐曲。在游戏中，与传说的宝可梦相遇的场景正好发生在湖畔，所以当我听到它被改编成那样时，内心深受感动。我觉得“其中蕴含着一种我无法表现出的神秘感”。另外一首是Disc
    2的第36首《206号道路（白天）》。这首曲子在一个叫206号道路的地方播放，实际上，它是我创作的第一首曲子。当它在《钻石&珍珠》第一集开头响起时，我高兴得几乎要哭出来。
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: It seems like the direction the arrangements take is what stimulates the
    sound team.
  translation: 编曲所选择的方向，似乎正是激发声音团队创作灵感的关键所在。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: You’re right.
  translation: 说得没错。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: Lastly, I’d like a small message from everyone.
  translation: 最后，请各位分别说几句话。
- speaker: 增田顺一
  original: Lately, I’ve done nothing but battle tracks and I feel bad about how they
    resemble each other (laughs), and since I’m slowly running out of ideas, I thought
    it would be nice to change directions at some point. To make Pokémon-like music
    with a new shape.
  translation: 最近我净是在写对战曲，自己也觉得它们彼此越来越像，实在有些过意不去（笑）。加上灵感也渐渐枯竭，便想着差不多该换个方向了。去创作一种形态崭新、却依然很“宝可梦”的音乐。
- speaker: 一之濑刚
  original: For a while, I’ve been thinking that, since “Pokémon” is a game millions
    of people play, the tracks included in it are heard by millions of people as well.
    In that regard, it’s a production which exerts a lot of influence. Even putting
    aside whether the same goes for the music, I’d prefer to do new things that differ
    from others. Concerning this CD, one of the things one should look forward to
    is hearing diverse endings (the last part of a track) that aren’t present in the
    games. I’d be happy if game fans as well as anime fans looked back at Pokémon
    music with fond memories when hearing live performances.
  translation: 一之濑刚：一直以来我都在思考，既然“宝可梦”是数百万人游玩的游戏，那么其中收录的乐曲自然也会被数百万人所聆听。从这层意义上来说，这是一部影响力巨大的作品。即便暂且不论音乐是否也是如此，我也更倾向于去做与他人不同的新尝试。关于这张CD，值得期待的一点便是能听到游戏中不曾有过的多样化的结尾（乐曲的收束部分）。如果游戏粉丝和动画粉丝在聆听现场演奏时，能够怀着美好的回忆回顾宝可梦的音乐，我会感到非常高兴。
- speaker: 佐藤仁美
  original: Among those who play the games, there might be some who aren’t very interested
    in music. I’d like to put something into the worldview of the tracks that makes
    those people think, “This is a nice piece” and that awakens their interest.
  translation: 在游玩游戏的玩家当中，或许有些人对音乐并不太感兴趣。我希望能在曲目的世界观中融入一些东西，让这些人也能觉得“这首曲子真不错”，从而唤起他们的兴趣。
- speaker: 宫崎慎二
  original: I might stray off a little, but I think it would be nice if we could have
    an event where people involved with Pokémon music gather.
  translation: 或许有些跑题，不过我觉得若能举办一场让与宝可梦音乐相关人士齐聚一堂的活动，那该多好。
- speaker: 采访者（Media Factory）
  original: It would be great to see that! Thank you for today.
  translation: 能听到这些真是太棒了！感谢各位今日莅临。
