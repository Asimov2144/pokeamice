- original: Tsukasa Tawada is a video game composer whose distinctive style and unforgettable melodies have earned him a place among the great composers in the Japanese video game scene. Best known for his work on series such as Pokémon1 and Dragon Quest2, Tawada’s breadth of work spans a variety of genres and covers multiple decades going back to the late ‘80s, when the Famicom (NES in North America) was the top-of-the-line video game console.
  translation: 多和田吏是一位电子游戏作曲家，其独特的风格与令人难忘的旋律为他赢得了日本游戏音乐界伟大作曲家之一的位置。他以在《宝可梦》1和《勇者斗恶龙》2等系列中的作品最为人熟知，其创作跨度涵盖多种类型，可追溯至20世纪80年代末——当时红白机（北美称NES）还是最顶级的游戏主机。
- original: '1: Sound direction, composition and arrangement of all tracks, recording, and technical implementation'
  quote: true
  translation: 1：负责全部曲目的音响指导、作曲与编曲、录音及技术实现
- original: '2: Sound direction and technical implementation for the main-series games, as well as arrangement of various tracks'
  quote: true
  translation: 2：负责正传游戏的音响指导与技术实现，以及多首曲目的编曲
- original: My first run-in with Tawada’s work occurred back in 2004, when Pokémon Colosseum was released internationally. For context, I was around 10 years old at the time and my reference for what a Pokemon game looked like was based on Ruby and Sapphire—the latest games in the series up to that point.
  translation: "我第一次接触到多和田的作品是在2004年，当时《宝可梦圆形竞技场》在国际上发行。说明一下背景：我那时大约10岁，我对宝可梦游戏样子的参照标准来自《红宝石／蓝宝石》——那是当时该系列最新的作品。"
- original: Then Pokémon Colosseum came and turned that reference on its head.
  translation: "然后《宝可梦圆形竞技场》出现了，彻底颠覆了我的参照标准。"
- original: Graphics aside, perhaps the most notable difference was in the game’s tone; instead of a bright world full of possibility and optimism, the player is thrust into a crime-ridden world filled with shady, run-down areas occupied by seedy characters who won’t hesitate to exploit Pokemon to their advantage.
  translation: 撇开画面不谈，最显著的不同或许在于游戏的基调：玩家被投入的不是一个充满可能性与乐观的明亮世界，而是一个犯罪横行、遍布阴暗破败之地、充斥着不择手段利用宝可梦谋取私利的卑劣角色的世界。
- original: And playing in the background, constantly reinforcing that darker, edgy tone is Tawada’s distinctive soundtrack, immersing the player through the entirety of their journey. I was hooked.
  translation: 而在背景中持续强化那种更黑暗、更前卫基调的，正是多和田独具特色的配乐，让玩家在整个旅程中始终沉浸其中。我被深深吸引了。
- original: 'Fast forward to 2012: by this time I’d developed a passionate hobby for covering video game tunes and sharing them on YouTube to some modest success. Given the impact that Pokémon Colosseum’s music had on me at the time, it’s no surprise that I chose to dig into Colosseum’s OST for new material to cover, starting with Cipher Peon Battle and its iconic guitar melody (that still gives me goosebumps to this day).'
  translation: "时间快进到2012年：那时我已经把翻奏游戏音乐并分享到YouTube发展成了一项充满热情的爱好，并取得了一些小小的成绩。鉴于《宝可梦圆形竞技场》的音乐当时对我产生的影响，我选择深入挖掘《圆形竞技场》的原声带寻找新的翻奏素材也就不足为奇了，首先从《暗影团手下战斗》及其标志性的吉他旋律开始（至今仍让我起鸡皮疙瘩）。"
- original: After uploading that video, I shared it with Tawada, and to my astonishment, actually received a response, and a heartfelt one at that, giving praise for the accuracy of the performance and offering words of encouragement. For a young musician like myself at the time, this was an invaluable gift which filled me with motivation to do even better and eventually led to more ambitious projects like the Pokémon Colosseum Medley, which in turn paved the way for even larger projects.
  translation: "上传那段视频后，我把它分享给了多和田，令我惊讶的是，我竟然收到了回复，而且是一封真诚的回复，他称赞了演奏的准确度，并给予了鼓励。对当时的我这样一个年轻音乐人来说，这是一份无价的礼物，让我充满动力去做得更好，并最终促成了像《宝可梦圆形竞技场组曲》这样更宏大的项目，而这又为更大的项目铺平了道路。"
- original: It was around that time that the idea to get together and play some of Tawada’s tunes live started getting floated around. I’m sure you can imagine the whirlwind of thoughts going through my mind around that idea.
  translation: "大约就在那个时候，开始有人提出大家聚在一起、现场演奏多和田的一些曲子的想法。你能想象这个想法在我脑中掀起了怎样的思绪风暴吗？"
- original: Being on opposite sides of the globe, however, made this tricky and the idea more or less lost steam…
  translation: 然而，身处地球两端让这件事变得棘手，这个想法也或多或少地不了了之了……
- original: 'Fast forward to 2025: at this point I’d been out of touch with Tawada for nearly a decade, but I’d finally decided to take the plunge and visit Japan. Given the amount of time that had passed, I had no expectation of meeting but figured I’d reach out anyway, letting him know of my upcoming trip. As luck would have it, he responded enthusiastically, agreeing to meet up and even going so far as to organize studio time for us to record in.'
  translation: 快进到2025年：此时我已经和多和田将近十年没有联系，但我终于下定决心去日本一趟。考虑到已经过去了这么久，我并没有指望能见面，但还是想着不妨联系一下，告诉他我即将到来的行程。幸运的是，他热情地回复了，同意见面，甚至主动为我们安排了录音室的录音时间。
- original: Towards the end of my trip in Japan, I met up with my friend and fellow video game musician Jonathan Parecki, who, having lived there for several years already, was a godsend in helping everything run smoothly and maintaining good vibes during what were a hectic few days of navigating the expansiveness of Tokyo in preparation for the session.
  translation: 在日本行程接近尾声时，我和我的朋友、同为电子游戏音乐人的Jonathan Parecki碰了面。他已经在日本生活了好几年，在那几天为准备录音而穿梭于东京广阔城区、忙得不可开交的日子里，他帮一切顺利进行、维持良好氛围，简直是天赐之助。
- original: In the end, what resulted was a surreal couple of days that I’ll never forget, which involved meeting up with the composer of some of mine and Jonathan’s favorite soundtracks, recording music together, and getting the opportunity to ask some burning questions that only Tawada himself could answer.
  translation: 最终的结果是难以忘怀、恍如梦境的两天：我们见到了我和Jonathan最喜爱的一些原声带的作曲者，一起录制音乐，还有机会问出一些只有多和田本人才能回答的迫切问题。

  translation: 以下是我们相处第一天“排练”环节之后进行的对话的修订版文字记录。

- original: 'Let’s start at the beginning: how did your relationship with music first develop, and how did that lead to you starting your journey as a musician?'
  role: question
  speaker: Dr. Pez
  translation: 我们从最开始聊起吧：你与音乐的关系最初是如何发展起来的，又是如何让你走上音乐人之路的？
- original: My father had a huge collection of records from all over the world, and there would always be music playing in the house. As such, I was exposed to all kinds of music from a young age. It wasn’t uncommon to hear pieces such as Beethoven’s symphonies playing first thing in the morning (laughs). That’s not to say I was only exposed to orchestral music though—I was listening to all kinds of music since infancy.
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 我父亲收藏了大量来自世界各地的唱片，家里总是放着音乐。因此，我从小就接触到各种各样的音乐。早上一起床就听到贝多芬交响曲这类作品并不稀奇（笑）。不过，这并不意味着我只接触管弦乐——我从小就听各种类型的音乐。
- original: Can you give us some specific examples of music playing in the house that caught your attention?
  role: question
  speaker: Dr. Pez
  translation: 能举几个具体例子，说说家里放的哪些音乐吸引了你的注意吗？
- original: I got to listen to all kinds of music - think genres such as chanson, Latin, jazz, classical, as well as folk music from Greece and other parts of the world. The first piece that really blew me away was Tchaikovsky’s Piano Concerto No. 1. The moment I heard that piece, I thought, “I’m going to play this myself!” (laughs)
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 我听过各种各样的音乐——比如香颂、拉丁、爵士、古典，还有来自希腊和世界其他地区的民间音乐。第一首真正让我震撼的曲子是柴可夫斯基的《第一钢琴协奏曲》。听到那首曲子的瞬间，我就想：“我要自己弹这首！”（笑）
- original: Looking back, that’s probably the first time I thought that I wanted to do music. Even though I used to hate piano lessons, I suddenly started playing again on my own and began practicing hard every day.
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 回头想想，那大概是我第一次觉得自己想做音乐。虽然我以前讨厌上钢琴课，但我突然又开始自己弹琴，并且每天拼命练习。
- original: Let’s zone in on Tchaikovsky’s Piano Concerto—when you eventually learned to play it, was it by sheet music or by ear?
  role: question
  speaker: Dr. Pez
  translation: 我们来聚焦柴可夫斯基的钢琴协奏曲——你最终学会弹它的时候，是靠乐谱还是靠耳朵听？
- original: It was way too complex for me at the time—there’s no way I could just figure it out by ear (laughs). I read the sheet music from cover to cover and analyzed it thoroughly.
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 当时对我来说太复杂了——我不可能光靠耳朵就把它弄明白（笑）。我把乐谱从头到尾读了一遍，仔细分析。
- original: The techniques that I picked up from learning classical music often come in handy when composing for games—for example, sometimes I use a contrapuntal approach, which appears frequently in Bach’s music. The structure is polyphonic which means there will be a distinct bass line and one or more upper voices all playing simultaneously.
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 我从学习古典音乐中学到的技巧，在为游戏作曲时常常派得上用场——比如，有时我会用对位法，这在巴赫的音乐中经常出现。其结构是复调的，也就是说会有一条清晰的低音线和一个或多个上声部同时演奏。
- original: Can you identify what about Piano Concerto No. 1 resonated with you so much?
  role: question
  speaker: Dr. Pez
  translation: 你能说说《第一钢琴协奏曲》的哪一点让你如此共鸣吗？
- original: The emotional depth and poignancy of the melodies in Tchaikovsky and Rachmaninoff’s music really struck a chord with me. It even made me want to study abroad in Moscow just to breathe the same air as them (laughs).
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 柴可夫斯基和拉赫玛尼诺夫音乐中旋律的情感深度与凄美，真的打动了我。它甚至让我想去莫斯科留学，只为和他们呼吸同样的空气（笑）。
- original: What I realized after experiencing that music was that I didn’t just want to listen to it —I wanted to play it myself.
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 在体验了那样的音乐之后，我意识到我不只是想听它——我想自己演奏它。
- original: Composing is a lot of fun for me, but jamming out, especially with fellow musicians, is even more fun!
  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 作曲对我来说很有趣，但即兴演奏，尤其是和其他音乐人一起，更有趣！

  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏

  role: question
  speaker: Dr. Pez

  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 比如说，我弹巴赫的时候会想：“我想把这里弹得更有节奏感一些。”

  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏

  role: answer
  speaker: 多和田吏
  translation: 做着做着，我渐渐萌生了一种愿望：创作自己的音乐，享受演奏它的乐趣。

  speaker: Dr. Pez
